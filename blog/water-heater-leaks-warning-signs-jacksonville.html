Water Heater Leaks: Warning Signs Jacksonville Homeowners Miss

October 2026 · 6 min read

Your water heater sits in the garage in Mandarin, quietly working for a decade. Then one morning you step into two inches of water. Water heater failures are among the most common sources of sudden flooding in Jacksonville homes, and they almost always give warning signs that go unnoticed.

How Long Do Water Heaters Last in Jacksonville?

In Jacksonville's hard water and high heat, traditional tank water heaters typically last 8 to 12 years. That is shorter than the national average, and the reason is our water chemistry. Minerals in North Florida water accelerate sediment buildup and tank corrosion. If your water heater is approaching 10 years old, it is living on borrowed time.

Garage installations, standard in most Jacksonville homes, expose water heaters to extreme temperature swings. Summer garage temperatures regularly exceed 100 degrees, which stresses tank components and accelerates wear on pressure relief valves and connections. The location that seemed convenient when the house was built becomes a liability as the unit ages.

Check the manufacture date on your unit's label. The first four digits of the serial number usually encode the month and year. If you cannot find records of when it was installed, this label tells you exactly how old it is. Anything past 10 years deserves a replacement plan, not just monitoring.

What Are the 6 Warning Signs of Failure?

Rusty or discolored hot water is the clearest early warning. When you first turn on the hot tap and the water runs brown or reddish, the inside of your tank is corroding. This does not fix itself. The corrosion will continue until the tank wall fails, and that failure is sudden.

Rumbling or popping sounds during heating cycles mean sediment has built up on the bottom of the tank. Jacksonville's mineral-rich water makes this especially common. The sediment layer causes overheating, weakens the tank bottom, and dramatically shortens remaining life. Annual flushing helps, but heavy buildup in an old unit is usually irreversible.

Moisture or small puddles around the base deserve immediate attention. Check the drain valve and all pipe connections first, since these can leak independently. But moisture with no visible source often means the tank itself is weeping through microscopic cracks. Place a dry paper towel around the base and check it daily. If it keeps getting damp, plan for replacement now.

Inconsistent water temperature or running out of hot water faster than usual signals a failing heating element or heavy sediment insulating the water from the burner. While not an emergency by itself, it indicates a unit in decline. Combined with age over 10 years, it means replacement should be on your near-term list.

A leaking temperature and pressure relief valve is a safety issue, not just a maintenance item. If the TPR valve drips constantly or the discharge pipe is wet, the valve may be failing or the tank may be overheating. Do not cap or plug this valve. Call a plumber promptly.

Visible corrosion on pipe connections, the tank exterior, or the burner assembly means the unit's protective systems are breaking down. Surface rust on fittings can sometimes be addressed, but widespread corrosion on the tank body itself means structural failure is approaching.

What Should You Do When a Water Heater Fails?

Shut off the water supply to the heater immediately using the cold water inlet valve on top of the unit. Then shut off power at the breaker for electric units or turn the gas control to pilot for gas units. These two steps stop the flooding and eliminate safety hazards.

Do not try to drain a failed heater yourself unless you know exactly what you are doing. A 50-gallon tank holds over 400 pounds of scalding water. Improper draining can cause burns or further flooding. Focus on containing the existing water with towels and moving valuables away from the wet area.

Call for professional water extraction quickly. Water heater failures dump dozens of gallons at once, and in Jacksonville's climate, that water starts feeding mold within a day. Garage installations often let water spread under walls into adjacent rooms where you cannot see it. Professional moisture detection finds the full extent before it becomes a bigger problem.
