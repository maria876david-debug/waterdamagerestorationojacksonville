Storm Damage Restoration — Jacksonville, FL

What counts as storm damage versus regular water damage?

Storm damage typically involves an external cause — wind-driven rain finding its way through a compromised roof, a tree limb puncturing a roofline, or storm surge pushing water into ground-floor areas. The restoration process (extraction, drying, documentation) is similar to any other water damage job, but storm damage often comes with structural repairs — roofing, siding — that go beyond water mitigation alone.

When is Jacksonville most at risk for storm damage?

Hurricane season runs June through November, with the highest activity typically between August and October. That said, severe thunderstorms with damaging wind can cause storm damage outside of named storm events too, particularly during Florida's summer storm patterns.

What should I do immediately after storm damage to my home?

Once it's safe, photograph the damage before any cleanup or temporary repairs, including the source of water intrusion (damaged roofing, broken windows, etc.). Temporary measures like tarping a roof can prevent further water entry, but keep records of any temporary repairs for your insurance claim. Call for a full assessment as soon as conditions allow safe access to the property.

Does insurance cover storm damage?

Wind-driven rain damage is often covered under a standard homeowners policy, since wind damage is typically a covered peril. However, if storm surge or flooding is the actual cause of water entry rather than wind and rain, that portion may fall under flood insurance instead — the cause of the water intrusion determines which coverage applies, and a single storm event can sometimes involve both.
