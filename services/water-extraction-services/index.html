Water Extraction — Jacksonville, FL

Water extraction is the first physical step in any restoration job — getting standing water out of the property as fast as possible, before it spreads further into flooring, walls, and subfloor.

What equipment is used for water extraction?

Submersible and truck-mounted pumps handle standing water more than an inch or two deep. Wet vacuums handle shallower water, carpet, and upholstery. For water that has already migrated into less visible areas, moisture meters and infrared cameras locate it before extraction equipment is positioned.

How is extraction different depending on the water source?

Clean water (a supply line break) can be extracted and the area dried without special disposal concerns. Grey water (an appliance overflow) requires more careful handling of any materials it touched. Black water (sewage or floodwater) requires disposal protocols for contaminated materials and disinfection of surfaces the water touched — extraction alone isn't the end of the process for contaminated water.

How fast does extraction need to happen?

As fast as possible. Water spreads through capillary action well beyond the visible wet area the longer it sits, and mold can begin forming within 24–48 hours. Extraction speed is the single biggest factor in whether a job stays a straightforward drying job or becomes a larger job involving material replacement and mold remediation.

Can extraction alone fix water damage?

No — extraction removes visible standing water, but structural drying and dehumidification are still needed afterward to address the moisture that's absorbed into building materials. Extraction is the first step of a multi-step process, not the whole process.
