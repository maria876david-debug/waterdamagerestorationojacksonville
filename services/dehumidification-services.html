Dehumidification Services — Jacksonville, FL

Dehumidification works alongside structural drying, but they're not the same thing — air movers push moisture out of materials into the air, and dehumidifiers are what actually remove that moisture from the air so it doesn't just resettle somewhere else in the home.

Why is dehumidification a separate step from drying?

Air movers accelerate evaporation from wet materials, but that moisture has to go somewhere — without dehumidifiers actively pulling it out of the air, it raises humidity throughout the space and can resettle into other materials, effectively spreading the problem rather than solving it. The two processes are run together for a reason.

Is dehumidification more important in Jacksonville than elsewhere?

Yes, meaningfully. Jacksonville's baseline outdoor humidity is already high for much of the year, particularly during the rainy season. That means the air inside an affected home has less natural capacity to hold additional moisture from a water-damaged structure without professional-grade dehumidification actively managing it.

What type of dehumidifiers are used?

Industrial low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers, which pull significantly more moisture from the air per hour than a residential unit — necessary for the volume of moisture involved in an active water damage job, not the kind of unit sold for a damp basement.

How long do dehumidifiers stay running?

Until moisture readings in the structure confirm the drying goal has been met — typically alongside the structural drying timeline of 3–5 days for a standard job, monitored rather than left running blindly.
