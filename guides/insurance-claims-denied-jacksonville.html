Why Water Damage Insurance Claims Get Denied in Jacksonville

Nearly 40% of water damage insurance claims were denied in 2024 — and in our experience, the same handful of reasons account for most of them. If you're dealing with water damage right now, understanding these upfront can be the difference between a paid claim and a denied one.

What's the single biggest reason claims get denied?

The "sudden vs. gradual" distinction. A pipe that bursts without warning is a sudden, accidental event — the kind standard homeowners policies are built to cover. A slow leak that's been dripping behind a wall for weeks or months is treated as gradual damage, which most policies exclude on the theory that ongoing maintenance should have caught it. Insurers scrutinize this distinction closely, and it's the line that most denials fall on.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?

No, not by default. Flood damage — water entering from outside the home due to rising groundwater, storm surge, or overwhelmed drainage — requires separate flood insurance, typically through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private carrier. Standard homeowners policies specifically exclude flood as a category, regardless of how the flooding happened.

What about sewage backup?

Also typically excluded unless you've added a specific sewage/water backup endorsement to your policy. This is one of the most commonly misunderstood gaps — homeowners assume "water damage" is one category their policy covers, when insurers actually split it into several distinct categories with different coverage rules.

How can I protect my claim from being denied?

  • Document immediately — photos and video of the damage before anything is moved, cleaned, or repaired
  • Identify and record the cause — knowing exactly what happened (burst pipe vs. slow leak vs. groundwater) shapes how the claim is classified
  • Report promptly — delays in reporting can be used to argue the damage worsened due to your inaction, which insurers may use to reduce or deny a payout
  • Keep records of prior maintenance — if you've had plumbing inspected or maintained, that documentation helps counter a "you should have known" argument
  • Get professional documentation — a restoration company's moisture readings, photos, and cause assessment carry more weight with adjusters than homeowner-taken photos alone

What should I do if my claim is already denied?

Request the denial in writing with the specific policy language cited. Review your policy directly rather than relying on a verbal explanation. You can request a re-inspection, provide additional documentation, or escalate to Florida's Department of Financial Services if you believe the denial was handled unfairly. A public adjuster (independent of your insurer) can also review the claim on your behalf, typically for a percentage of any recovered amount.
